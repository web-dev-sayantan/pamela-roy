Most people who come to me do not have a writing problem. They have a time problem and a clarity problem wearing a writing problem's coat, and the first two are easier to solve than the last one.

## What I actually do

I interview you properly, several times, over weeks. I write down what you say, including the parts you say sideways. Then I give you a structure — chapters, argument, order — before there is a draft, so that the disagreements happen cheaply and early rather than at the point where they are expensive.

Then I write it, in your voice or a version of it we agree on in advance, and you read the drafts, and we talk about them. That is the process. It is not glamorous and it works.

## What I will not do

I will not write a book about a subject you know nothing about, and I will not invent quotations, anecdotes or statistics. If the book needs a fact and nobody has the fact, we go and find it. If the book needs you to remember something and you cannot, we leave the space empty and put it in front of you to fill.

I will also tell you, early, if the book is not there. About one conversation in six ends with me recommending a shorter book, an article, or nothing yet. That is not a lost project; it is the cheaper outcome, discovered in an hour rather than after six weeks of both our time.

## How it runs

We start with one conversation, free, and it either goes somewhere or it does not. If it does, I put a proposal in writing: scope, stages, dates, fee, and what is excluded. You have a week to read it and change anything in it.

Most projects are one book. Some clients come back, and one or two have stayed for years, which I like more than I should.
