This is a working note rather than a piece, so it will be short and slightly listy, and I will not dress it up.

The method, on one page:

1. A walk of at least an hour, on a route I know well enough that I do not have to think about it.
2. A notebook, physical, no phone. The phone is the single most important variable and I have proved this to myself several times.
3. About a hundred words an hour. Not an estimate. Roughly a page, and the page is full when the page is full.
4. Nothing added afterwards. The notebook is a record, not a manuscript, and the moment I start tidying it the day is over.

That is the whole thing. Everything else I do is bookkeeping about those four hours.

## Why the route has to be known

A familiar route is a controlled experiment. Every variable is the same except the day. If the notebook is better on a Tuesday than it is on a Thursday, the difference is the day, because the path, the weather, the shoes and the hour were all already accounted for.

An unfamiliar route is a different tool entirely. It gives you material, and material is not the same as thinking, and I confuse them regularly.

## Why a hundred words

Because it is small enough to do when everything else has gone wrong. On the days when the work is impossible and the day has already contained two difficult conversations, a hundred words is a thing that can be done. And then the walk is long enough that there is room for the rest of it, which is usually the part where the actual sentence arrives at minute fifty-five, when I have stopped trying.

> Attention is not a resource you spend. It is a room you keep tidying, and it stays clean or it does not.

## Why nothing is added afterwards

Because the notebook is not the piece. If I go back and improve it, I am doing the work twice, and the second pass will be the correct thing rather than the true thing, and the true thing is the only one worth keeping.

I do transcribe, eventually, into a document. Transcription is a separate activity on a separate day and it is mostly cutting. What survives the cut is usually one line in ten, and the one in ten is almost never the line I would have chosen at the time, because at the time I was listening and now I am selecting, and listening is better at this.

The rest of the year is mostly other people's deadlines. The four hours are the part that is mine.
