Working notes. Not ready, not for the site yet.

The premise: a rejected manuscript does not disappear, it goes somewhere, and what happens to it is worth writing about. Forty books a year go out and come back. Where do they go?

- Some go straight into a second attempt. The same book, the same writer, two versions. Rareer than people think, and interestingly the worst books often get rewritten first, because the writer is still in the room with them.
- Some are the *good* book that was written badly. A book that was ready in draft four and was submitted at draft one. This is the most common category of regret and nobody talks about it in public.
- Some are correct. A short book with no audience at that moment. A book that needed a different reader.

The research question I have not answered: is there any way to find out? I have a reader-friends account, a filing system with a boolean flag, and a genuine lack of evidence. This might be the whole essay — the difficulty of looking at your own decisions later.

Open threads:

- The word *rejected* does a lot of damage and is nearly always inaccurate.
- Failure of a first book and failure of a tenth book are not the same event and should not be described in the same sentence.
- The Henry James letter problem. Overwriting the dedication on the strength of a bad review. Needs a source.
- Ask S. whether she still has her rejections in a drawer. If she does, bring the drawer.
