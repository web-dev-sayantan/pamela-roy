There is a shelf above the radiator in the front room that is not, in any ordinary sense, a library. It holds seventeen books, and the seventeen is stable, and I have never in seven years added an eighteenth without removing one.

The reason is not sentiment and it is not space. It is that the shelf is a tool, and a tool with no limit stops being a tool.

## The limit is the point

A collection without a limit is a heap. This is not a moral observation; it is a functional one. A stack of ninety books tells you nothing about what you are interested in, because the answer is *everything you have ever been handed*. Fifty is no better. Seventeen, maintained deliberately, is an answer.

When I add a book, the question is not *do I like this*. It is *what does this displace*. If I cannot answer that, the book is not going on the shelf. It goes on the table by the bed, where there is already a heap of things I liked very much and did not displace anything, which is a different category entirely.

> A collection is a set of decisions. A heap is a set of acquisitions. Only one of them tells you anything about its owner.

The mechanics, and I do recommend attempting them:

1. A hard number. Seventeen here. A number you will not renegotiate.
2. One out, one in. Never both in the same week.
3. The removed book goes to a real destination, not a shelf somewhere else in the house. The charity shop, the second-hand bookshop, a person who has said they would like it. Otherwise you have only moved the problem, and you will find out in about two years.
4. A written reason, kept in a notebook, for anything you remove. The reasons are the only part of the collection that is genuinely yours. The titles are available to anyone.

## What it is actually for

The shelf is not for storage. Storage is what the box under the bed is for, and the box under the bed is much larger and much worse.

It is for the thing I could not do any other way, which is to walk into a room and see, in about four seconds, the entire shape of what I have been thinking about for seven years. I cannot produce that list in advance. I could not have chosen those seventeen books in 2017. I can only look at them now and say: yes, that is it, that is the shape of it, and here is why this one had to go.

That is what a library is. Not a record of what you own. A record of what you were trying to work out.
